How the text sample was selected and coded

From 706 records with at least one nonblank Q5 answer, 60 were selected without replacement using pandas sample(n=60, random_state=42). All three Q5 fields were read: 153 nonblank answers in total. Each record was assigned manually reviewed, overlapping topic codes once per theme. These are assistant-assisted exploratory codes, not independently validated qualitative measurements.

The coding drew on the earlier exploratory review. One record contained only place names and was left unassigned. The ambiguous answer “Death” was included under human impacts; excluding it reduces that theme to 15/60 (25.0%). Row references count the spreadsheet header as row 1.

Counterexamples: row 111 lists both “Excessive cold” and “Excessive heat,” complicating a warming-only interpretation. Row 250 gives only “War,” foregrounding a social concern. No explicit rejection of concern appeared in this sample; this does not establish its absence from the full dataset.

Interpretation boundary. These results describe the people who answered this survey. The sample’s geographic concentration, small subgroups, and brief text responses limit claims about worldwide priorities or causal explanations.

03 / POTENTIAL A/B TEST

From expressed concern
to a measurable action.

Proposed study · not conducted. The survey suggests both ecological and human concerns matter. A future experiment could test whether message framing changes engagement with coastal conservation information.

Hypothesis: A community-focused message will produce a higher click-through rate to a coastal conservation guide than an ecosystem-focused message. The survey motivates this hypothesis; it does not establish which message will work better.

Version A / Ecosystem framing

Protect the life along our coasts.

Healthy coastal habitats support marine plants and animals. Explore ways to help protect these ecosystems.

Explore coastal conservation

Version B / Community framing

Protect the communities along our coasts.

Healthy coastal habitats support people’s wellbeing and livelihoods. Explore ways to help protect these ecosystems.

Explore coastal conservation

Illustrative message mockups. The call-to-action previews above are not live experiment buttons.

Randomize the message

Assign eligible visitors 1:1 at the visitor level and keep their assignment consistent. Change only the framing copy; hold layout, imagery, button wording, destination, and exposure conditions constant.

Measure engagement

Primary outcome: the proportion of exposed visitors who click the guide link at least once during their first session. Report the absolute percentage-point difference and a 95% confidence interval. A click is an engagement proxy, not evidence of sustained conservation behavior.

Decide before launch

Use a pilot baseline and a meaningful minimum effect to calculate sample size (two-sided α=.05, 80% power). Predefine eligibility, bot exclusions, duration, and stopping rules. Check assignment balance and technical failures before interpreting results.

What would count as a useful result?

Report results even if inconclusive. Evaluate both uncertainty and whether the effect is large enough to matter; do not select a winner simply because its observed rate is higher. Any subgroup analysis by country or ocean-user group should be prespecified or clearly labeled exploratory.

A subsequent study could measure a stronger outcome, such as completing a conservation activity. Survey associations and this proposed click experiment answer different questions. This portfolio does not assign visitors to variants or collect experiment data.

Analysis approach

Descriptive summaries, codebook-based labeling, respondent-level percentages, and a documented sample review of open text. Multiple-choice selections are treated as selections, not rankings. “I don’t know” and nonresponse categories remain distinct.

The portfolio contains aggregate results and selected excerpts. The A/B test is a proposal; no experimental outcomes are presented.
